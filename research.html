Research interests

I work at the interface of analytical relativity, black-hole physics, and gravitational-wave astronomy. My research connects the dynamics and geometry of compact binaries with the waveforms we model and the signals we observe, using these connections to test gravity and understand merging black holes.

A connecting thread is the evolution of a binary through inspiral, merger, and ringdown: from post-Newtonian dynamics and waveform construction to dynamical horizons and black-hole spectroscopy. I combine analytical methods, numerical-relativity information, and gravitational-wave data analysis to study different aspects of this process.

Post-Newtonian theory and compact-binary dynamics

I study the analytical description of compact binaries, including their multipolar structure, orbital dynamics, and gravitational radiation.

Gravitational-wave modelling and machine learning

I develop waveform models linking the inspiral, merger, and ringdown of compact binaries. My recent work uses Kolmogorov–Arnold networks to learn corrections to a post-Newtonian baseline from numerical-relativity waveforms, then represents those corrections as explicit splines.

Tests of general relativity

I use gravitational waves to probe gravity in the strong-field, dynamical regime. My interests include parameterized tests of the multipolar structure of compact binaries, tests using multiband observations, and the modelling and data-analysis systematics that affect these measurements.

Black-hole spectroscopy and ringdown

I investigate the quasi-normal modes of black holes and what ringdown observations can tell us about their properties and departures from the Kerr description. My work also explores the physical significance of mode-spectrum instability and the systematics involved in interpreting ringdown signals.

Dynamical horizons and black-hole mergers

I also study black holes through the evolution of their horizons during a merger. This work investigates the dynamical geometry of merging black holes, including cusp formation, alongside my research on the gravitational radiation they emit.

Gravitational-wave astrophysics

I explore the astrophysical information carried by gravitational-wave observations, from compact-binary catalogues and the evolution of merger populations to stochastic backgrounds. My work includes the 3-OGC and 4-OGC catalogues, neutron-star merger-rate evolution, and gravitational-wave backgrounds from exoplanets.
